PlanFetch

Privacy Policy

Effective November 20, 2026. PlanFetch was called mySync until that day; nothing else in this policy changed.

The short version

  • Apple Intelligence reads your messages on your iPhone, with Apple's on-device model only, never Private Cloud Compute or any model in the cloud.
  • PlanFetch has no servers, accounts, analytics or ads. Its developer never receives your messages, calendar or contacts.
  • A few things do leave your iPhone, each for a reason listed below.

What PlanFetch reads

Only what you turn on or hand to it:

  • Texts, through a shortcut you add. It hands PlanFetch your recent messages, both sent and received, each time it runs.
  • Email in Apple Mail, through a shortcut you add.
  • Email accounts you connect. PlanFetch reads new mail arriving in the inbox from the moment you connect, never the mail already there, sent mail or other folders.
  • Notifications from apps you set up, such as a chat app, through a shortcut you add for that app.
  • Screenshots, if you turn them on, and what's on screen when you use Back Tap.
  • What you share or paste into PlanFetch.

A source that's off reads nothing.

Where it's read

Apple Intelligence's on-device model reads your messages, and Apple's Vision framework reads the text in images, both on your iPhone. PlanFetch doesn't use Private Cloud Compute or any model behind a server of its own.

If you choose the Local Model in Settings or in first run, PlanFetch downloads it once from Hugging Face, which sends nothing of yours, and then reads your messages with it on your iPhone. Apple Intelligence stays the default.

What leaves your iPhone

Place names, to Apple Maps

A place or address written in a message goes to Apple Maps to find its address, with a city named in the message or the home area you set, if any. PlanFetch never uses your iPhone's location.

Your email login, to your email provider

An account you connect is checked directly with its provider, using the app password you give it. To find the mail server for a company's address, the iPhone looks up that address's domain in DNS. The page for making an app password is your provider's own website.

Events you add, to the calendar you pick

An event's title, time, place and notes go into that calendar, and its notes carry a quote of at most 500 characters from the message and a link back. If the calendar syncs with a service such as iCloud, Google or Outlook, they go there too, and apps you allow into your calendar can see them. A reminder you add goes into Reminders with a link to PlanFetch, not the message's words.

Events you share, to whoever you send them

Share puts an event's title, day and time, place and an online meeting's link in a message for you to send, never the message it came from. Its link opens this website's Add to Calendar page with the event in the part of the link after #, which the browser keeps on the phone that opens it and never sends to this site. Nothing goes until you tap Send.

Your subscription, to Apple

The App Store handles purchases and subscription status.

What stays on your iPhone

  • A message's full text is deleted once it's read. A message waiting to be read stays until then.
  • The conversation around a text. The newest 40 texts of a conversation, both sides, yours included, at most 500 characters each, stay for up to 3 days so later texts can be read in context. They're never shown.
  • Each plan found, with its quote of at most 500 characters, its suggested replies, a link back to the source, who sent it and when. They stay until you use Delete All Data.
  • Activity lists each message read, who sent it, when, and what came of it, but not what it said. It also stays until you use Delete All Data.
  • A record of what's been read, so nothing is read twice: a mail or screenshot's ID, and for a text or notification a fingerprint that can't be turned back into its words, forgotten after a day.
  • Small files for the widget and the PlanFetch app in Messages: for the widget, your upcoming events' titles, places and times; for the Messages app, when you're busy (start and end only) and, for plans from the last 2 days, each one's title, day and time, the sender's first name and your suggested replies.
  • Passwords and keys are kept only in the iPhone's Keychain.

The texts, quotes and replies PlanFetch keeps are encrypted with a key that exists only in this iPhone's Keychain, and iOS keeps each app's data from other apps. If your iPhone backs up, its backup can include PlanFetch's data, but that key doesn't move to another iPhone, so what's encrypted can't be read there.

What it asks to use

  • Calendars (full access): to add the events you confirm, check for duplicates and overlaps, and show your schedule. Your other events aren't copied, apart from the widget's and the Messages app's small files above.
  • Notifications: to tell you about new plans, on your iPhone and a paired Apple Watch.
  • Photos (full access, only if you turn Screenshots on): to read the text of screenshots taken after you turned it on. It doesn't load other photos or keep any image.
  • Contacts (only when you tap Open in Messages for a name Messages gave): to find that person's number. Nothing from your contacts is kept or leaves your iPhone.
  • Reminders (only when you tap Add as Reminder): to add that reminder. Nothing is read back.
  • The clipboard: iOS tells PlanFetch whether what you copied looks like a plan, without PlanFetch reading it. It's read only if you tap Paste.

What the developer receives

Nothing from the app. PlanFetch has no servers, and it contains no analytics, advertising, attribution or crash-reporting code. It doesn't track you, and the developer doesn't sell or share your data.

Apple gives every app's developer sales and download figures and overall usage statistics, and crash reports from people who turned on Share With App Developers in iOS Settings › Privacy & Security › Analytics & Improvements. They don't include your messages.

If you email support, your email and whatever you put in it are used only to answer you. This website has no analytics or cookies, though its hosting provider may keep ordinary server logs.

Your choices

  • Turn off a source with its switch in the Sources tab. You can also withdraw a permission in iOS Settings at any time.
  • Delete everything with Settings › Delete All Data. It removes plans, history, quotes, connected accounts, the widget's and Messages app's files, and the encryption key. Events and reminders you've already added stay in your calendar and Reminders, where you can delete them yourself.
  • Before deleting the app, use Delete All Data, because iOS can keep Keychain items after an app is deleted. Deleting the app doesn't cancel a subscription.

The developer holds none of your messages, so there's nothing to request or erase from a server of ours. For a copy or deletion of what your email provider, calendar service or Apple holds, ask them. For any privacy question, write to mysync.support@gmail.com.

Children

PlanFetch isn't directed to children under 13, and it doesn't knowingly collect anyone's personal information.
